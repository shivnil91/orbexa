# Positioning

Orbexa is where real experts' know-how becomes AI you can trust.

A buyer should not have to wonder whether a listing is a prompt someone typed into a general model. Every agent has to do three things:

1. Prove it beats plain AI on realistic tasks for its category.
2. Be backed by a named person who stands behind it.
3. Carry a track record from paid jobs, once those jobs exist.

That is the product. A prompt box is not.

## What a buyer can check

**Knowledge, without seeing the files.** The seller adds templates, checklists, past examples, and rules of thumb, and answers five to seven "how I'd do it" questions. The listing shows the counts and the types. It does not show the text. At run time the worker retrieves from that seller's material only. The material is fenced as untrusted data, the same way a buyer document is fenced. It is not used for training unless the seller turns that on. The default is off.

**Orbexa Proof.** On publish, and when the seller's material changes, the harness builds 10 held-out tasks for the category and also grades the seller's own examples. Each task is run twice on the same model: once with the seller's instructions and private material, once with neither. A separate judge scores the two drafts without being told which is which, and the order is randomised. The listing shows the win rate, the rubric scores, two side-by-side drafts, the test date, and the version. A win rate of 60% or below is labelled **Not proven** and sorts below agents that beat the baseline. With no model key, the harness uses a deterministic fixture and says so. A fixture result is not a customer statistic.

**A named expert.** The profile shows a name, a photo or initials, an experience statement, links, and credentials. A credential is **Self-declared** until an operator runs the manual check. The check writes an audit log. Nothing in the product says **Verified** without that check. Example listings say they are example data.

**A guarantee.** If the buyer rejects a draft and gives a reason, they get one free revision. If they reject that revision with a reason, the refund goes through the reviewed refund path: an operator approves it, and the approval is idempotent. An optional expert-review tier lets the seller edit the draft before delivery, at a higher price, with an SLA the seller sets.

**A track record from paid jobs only.** Completed jobs, acceptance, revisions, repeat buyers, median delivery time, and reviews tied to a paid accepted order. Empty means no paid jobs yet. Seed listings stay labelled as example data.

Search rank is relevance, times the proof win rate, times the paid-job track record. Proven agents sort above the rest. The match shows the Proof badge, how many of the held-out tests the agent won, and the expert's name.

## Beachheads

The first US beachheads are kinds of work where a plain model is likely to fill a gap the file does not support, and where a specialist already has a checklist for that failure. These are reasons, not market statistics. No size, share, or win-rate claim is made here.

### US government and state bid responses

A usable response follows the solicitation's form and evaluation factors. When the supplier file does not contain a certificate, a price, or a signer, the row has to stay open. A general model is pulled toward a smooth narrative and will often supply the missing fact. A specialist's matrix, a past response, and an explicit rule against inventing a certificate change the draft. Tender writing is already the first pack in this repo, and the hospital-bed fixture is an example of that shape: a supported dimension can be cited, and a missing certificate stays missing. That fixture is example data.

### Insurance claim appeals

An appeal is the denial reason, the policy language that is actually in the file, and the records that are not. A persuasive letter that cites an exclusion or a benefit the file does not contain is worse than a short list of gaps. A specialist checklist — denial code, cited page, missing record — is what makes the draft safe to hand to a person. The listing is not a law firm and the draft is not legal advice.

### Local permit and code checklists

Completeness is local: which sheet, which calculation, which stamp line, which dimension is absent. A general model does not have that city's checklist, and it will guess a number rather than leave the line blank. The specialist's checklist and the rule "never invent a measurement" produce the missing items. That is the work product.

Other categories stay on the marketplace. These three are where the difference between a prompt and a specialist's files is easiest to see.

## What this does not claim

No certification, no invented customer count, no logo, and no statement that a fixture proof is a live-model result. A credential is not verified until the operator check is in the audit log. A new agent has no paid-job track record until someone pays and the job finishes.
