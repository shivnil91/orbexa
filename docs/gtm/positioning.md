# Positioning

Orbexa is the platform for AI twins in high-stakes professions. A specialist builds a twin that can outperform plain AI, and that can be measured against their own past cases. They deploy that twin on any channel: an API, a plug-in for other AI tools, an embed on their own website, email, messaging, socials, and the Orbexa marketplace. Orbexa runs it, secures it, bills it, and distributes it.

The marketplace is one channel. It is not the product. The product is the studio, the sealed agent package, and the runtime that every channel calls.

A twin is not a prompt pasted into a general model. It has the specialist's scope, intake, hard rules, calculators, playbooks, templates, and the point where the work stops and a person takes the deal.

## What the homepage says

**Hero:** Build the AI twin of your expertise. Prove it beats plain AI. Deploy it everywhere.

**Under the hero:** Specialists in finance and compliance build a twin that follows their rules, cites every claim, and is measured against plain AI and their own past cases. Orbexa runs, secures, bills, and distributes that twin. The marketplace is one channel. An API, an embed, email, and messaging call the same runtime. On a large or complex deal the twin stops and hands the work to the specialist.

The $15 figure still appears on the cheapest example job on the marketplace shelf (a customer reply). It is a shelf price in this demo, not the definition of the product. Example listings and example ratings are labelled. With no model key, a paid draft is written on this server from the listing. With a key, the runtime calls that model, with untrusted text fenced as data. It is not a certified professional service.

Sellers keep 80% of a marketplace job's starting price when a buyer accepts the draft. Orbexa keeps 20%. This demo does not send a bank payout. A broker's own model is the agent fee plus a qualified lead when the twin hands the deal over. Nothing here is fine-tuned on the specialist's files. Training use stays off unless they turn it on, and this demo does not train a model either way.

## Who it is for

**Primary customer.** The specialist. The first wedges are finance and compliance: lending and broking, including disability-provider finance; corporate finance; AML/CTF; insurance claims; and government bids.

The reference case is a business loan broker who is strong in disability service provider (NDIS) loans and equity. Their client is often an accounting firm. The broker builds the twin. The firm hires it, completes an intake that can be shared with the provider, and receives a funding note, a lender-ready pack, and a missing-documents checklist. The note is labelled indicative, not a credit offer. Example lender names on that listing are example data.

**Not the first customer.** A creator who wants a chat clone of their public content. A team that wants a generic no-code agent for an internal FAQ. Those are real products. They are not this one.

A buyer on the marketplace is still served. They name a job, pick a match, and pay. That path remains. It is a distribution channel for a twin that already exists.

## The moat

These are product choices, not measured market shares.

- **Deterministic rules and calculators.** Hard knock-outs (trading history, revenue, credit, tax debt, and the equivalents in another domain) run in code. The model does not get to invent a pass. Calculators show the formula and the assumptions.
- **Measured Proof.** The same model answers with the specialist's material and as plain AI without it. The specialist's own past cases are a separate bench, with a pass or fail and a diff. A live win rate under 70%, a bench that is not all hard-rule cases, or citation coverage under 90% does not unlock "Publish as Proven". A stand-in scorer is labelled as a stand-in.
- **Per-client context.** Each end client has their own documents, answers, and memory. A run names that client. Another specialist does not see it.
- **Citations on every claim.** A sentence cites a rule, a calculator, a playbook, or a document, or it is marked as model reasoning. Coverage is shown.
- **Handoff.** Deal size, equity, and complexity stop the twin and book the human. That is how the specialist gets paid for the work a model should not close.
- **Regulated-grade trust.** Seller material is fenced. A draft that tries to steer the judge is dropped. Spend caps are taken inside the lock. Uploads are size-limited. Training consent defaults to off.

## Where a specialist still beats a plain model

The shape of the work is the reason, not a market size. A plain model fills a gap the file does not support. A twin that has the specialist's rules leaves the gap and says which rule fired.

**Lending and broking, including disability-provider finance.** The file is registration, revenue mix, payment lag, debtor days, property exposure, payroll, existing debt, and the tax position. The output is a ranked structure, an indicative size, and the documents still missing. Equity and a large amount hand the file to the broker.

**Corporate finance, AML/CTF, and insurance claims.** The useful draft stays inside the file: the denial reason that is present, the clause that is present, the check that failed. A persuasive paragraph that cites a benefit or a clearance the file does not contain is a failure.

**Government bids.** A response follows the solicitation. A missing certificate stays missing. The hospital-bed listing in this repo is example data for that shape. It is not a live procurement offer.

## Competitive set

Written from public descriptions. No user counts, revenue, win rates, or valuations are added here.

**Delphi.** Sequoia-backed. It builds digital minds for creators and coaches from their content. The experience is conversational. That is a different job from a regulated file with hard knock-outs and a cited calculation.

**Kopai.** A no-code agent cloud with an API and a marketplace. The overlap is distribution. The difference is that Orbexa's package is a specialist's rules and proof, not a general builder.

**Squidgy.** An agent builder with multi-channel distribution. Its published fee is 25%. Orbexa's marketplace demo keeps 20% of a job's starting price. The fee is not the product difference. The difference is deterministic rules, proof against plain AI and against the specialist's own cases, and a handoff on the deals the human must own.

**Kora.** An agent-to-agent expertise API. Calling another agent's expertise is a real interface. Orbexa's runtime is the place a twin runs, with the specialist's rules applied in code and a trace on the answer.

**Custom GPTs and the labs' own assistants.** A custom assistant is a prompt, sometimes with files, on that lab's model. It is not graded against the same model without the files, it does not apply a knock-out table in code, and it does not hand a deal back to a named broker with a citation trail.

**Where Orbexa should win.** High-stakes work where a wrong figure or a missing citation is the failure, where the specialist can write the rule, and where the buyer needs to see that the twin beat plain AI and the specialist's own cases.

**Where Orbexa should not compete.** Chat clones of a creator. Generic no-code builders for teams that do not have a domain rule to enforce. Those products are built for a conversation. This one is built for a decision a person can check.

## What this does not claim

No certification. No invented customer count. No claim that a stand-in proof is a live-model result. No claim that email, messaging, socials, or an embed adapter is a separate shipped app in this demo. The runtime accepts one channel request and returns one channel response, so those adapters can call it. They are not built in this repository yet. A credential is not verified until the operator check is in the audit log. A new twin has no paid-job track record until someone pays and the job finishes. Lender names, criteria, and rates on the disability-finance example are example data, labelled "Example data: replace with your panel".
